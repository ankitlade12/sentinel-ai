# Form I-90, Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card

Form I-90 is used to renew or replace a Permanent Resident Card (Green Card,
Form I-551). You generally file Form I-90 if your existing card has expired or
will expire within the next six months, or if it was lost, stolen, damaged, or
contains incorrect information.

## There is no 30-day deadline to file

There is **no 30-day filing window** and no fixed deadline that, if missed,
causes you to lose status. You may file Form I-90 when your Green Card is
expiring or has already expired. Filing is about keeping valid evidence of your
status current — it is not a deadline that ends your status.

**Important:** Your status as a lawful permanent resident does **not** end when
your Green Card expires. An expired card is an expired *document*; it is not a
loss of permanent residence. You remain a permanent resident, but you should
renew the card so you have valid evidence of that status for work and travel.

## When to file

- Your card has expired or will expire within six months: file to renew.
- Your card was lost, stolen, or destroyed: file to replace.
- Your name or other biographic information has changed: file to replace.

## Filing and fees

You can file Form I-90 online or by mail. After filing, USCIS issues a receipt
notice that, together with your expired card, can serve as temporary evidence of
your status. Fee amounts and any fee-waiver eligibility are listed on the
official USCIS Form I-90 page; confirm the current fee there before filing.

> Conditional permanent residents (2-year card) generally do **not** use Form
> I-90 to renew — they file Form I-751 or Form I-829 to remove conditions. Use
> Form I-90 for the standard 10-year card.
