# Tenant Rights: Notices, Evictions, and Security Deposits

This chapter summarizes common tenant protections. Exact notice periods and
procedures vary by state and locality; this handbook reflects the rules the
clinic's service area follows. Always confirm the rule for the client's specific
jurisdiction.

## Notice before eviction

A landlord generally cannot remove a tenant without first giving written notice
and then going through a court process. Common notice types:

- **Nonpayment of rent:** the landlord must give a written notice (commonly
  **14 days** in our service area) giving the tenant a chance to pay before an
  eviction case can be filed.
- **Lease violation (curable):** written notice giving the tenant an opportunity
  to fix the problem.
- **No-cause termination (month-to-month):** a written termination notice,
  commonly **30 days**, and longer in some localities or for longer tenancies.

A landlord may **not** use "self-help" eviction — changing the locks, removing a
tenant's belongings, or shutting off utilities to force a tenant out is illegal.
Only a court can order an eviction, carried out by a sheriff or marshal.

## Security deposits

After a tenancy ends, the landlord must return the security deposit, minus
lawful deductions for unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear, within
the period set by state law (often **14 to 30 days**). The landlord typically
must provide an itemized statement of any deductions.

## Habitability

Landlords must maintain rental units in a habitable condition (working heat,
plumbing, and safe conditions). Tenants have remedies if a landlord fails to
make required repairs, but the available remedies and any notice requirements
depend on local law.
