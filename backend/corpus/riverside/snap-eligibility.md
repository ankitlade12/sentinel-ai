# SNAP Eligibility and Income Limits

The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) helps low-income households
buy food. Eligibility depends mainly on household size, gross and net monthly
income, and certain resources.

## Gross monthly income limit (130% of the federal poverty level)

For the federal fiscal year in effect, most households must have gross monthly
income at or below **130%** of the poverty line. Approximate gross monthly
limits by household size:

| Household size | Gross monthly income limit |
|---|---|
| 1 | $1,632 |
| 2 | $2,215 |
| 3 | $2,798 |
| 4 | $3,380 |
| Each additional member | + $583 |

Households with an elderly (60+) or disabled member may be subject to the net
income test (100% of poverty) instead of the gross income test, and different
rules can apply.

## Other factors

- **Net income:** after allowable deductions (housing, dependent care, medical
  for elderly/disabled), net monthly income must generally be at or below 100%
  of the poverty line.
- **Resources:** countable resources (such as cash) are subject to a limit,
  which is higher for households with an elderly or disabled member.
- **Work requirements:** able-bodied adults without dependents may face
  time-limited benefits unless they meet work requirements or an exemption.

These figures are updated annually (each October). Confirm the current
household-size limits on the official USDA SNAP page before advising a client,
because outdated thresholds are a common source of error.
